Questions we expect

Straight answers, including the things we do not have yet.

Who we are

Is Ovalfort a registered company?

Not yet. Ovalfort is an early-stage project run by Rahul Phaltankar, an individual, in the United Kingdom. If it moves into a company, we will tell you in writing first, and you may end the beta at that point. The beta terms say this at the top.

Why do you use a gmail address?

It is a dedicated inbox for the project, and a company domain will follow incorporation. Until then you can check who is behind it: Rahul Phaltankar's profile and LinkedIn page show twenty years delivering technology and regulatory change for banks and insurers. We would rather you check than take our word.

Paperwork

Can we use our own NDA?

Yes. Send it to us. If it is an ordinary mutual agreement, we will sign it in place of the confidentiality section in ours.

Can our lawyers review these terms?

Please do. The terms are in plain language, and a solicitor review is in progress. The page says so. You can print the page to PDF. Send any changes to rahulregtech@gmail.com and we will tell you plainly what we can accept.

How do we accept, and when does it start?

Fill in the form on the terms page and tick three boxes. It prepares an email to us with your acceptance, the version, the time and a fingerprint of the exact text. The agreement is made, and the 30 days start, when we reply to confirm. That email exchange is the record.

What you do and what you get

What do you need from us?

Your company name and public website, about 15 to 30 minutes to answer 22 guided questions (“not sure” is a fair answer), and, if you wish, redacted documents such as policies. We do not need source code, production databases, personal health data, passwords or keys, and we ask you not to send them.

What do we get at the end?

A written reading, law by law, across the EU AI Act, EU GDPR, UK GDPR and India's DPDP and CERT-In rules, with the official wording behind every finding, plus a spreadsheet of findings ordered by seriousness. A signed reading is sealed, so any later change shows.

Can we show the reading to investors, customers or authorities?

Yes. The reading and fix list are yours to use as you wish. It is a preliminary view, and we ask you to describe it that way. It is not a certificate, and it does not stop an authority reaching its own view.

Is this legal advice? Does it say we are compliant?

No to both. It is a preliminary view of likely obligations and gaps from public information and what you tell us. It never says a company is compliant. Take legal advice before acting on a finding.

Is the India part reviewed by an Indian lawyer?

Not yet. The India part is built and sourced from the official texts, and every reading says that an Indian legal review is pending. Treat it accordingly.

What if the reading finds something serious? Who else sees it?

Only you. We do not report to anyone, we will not name you publicly without your written permission, and if the law ever forces us to disclose something we will tell you first where the law allows.

Your data

Where is our data kept?

In a private workspace in Google Cloud's London region, separate from every other company's workspace. Google Cloud is our only sub-processor. Google's build and logging tools are managed by Google and are not tied to London.

Do you use AI on our data?

No. The deep dive calls no AI model. Our own code reads your evidence against a stored copy of the law, and separate code checks that each quotation exists in the official text. Nothing you provide is sent to an AI service. If that ever changes, we tell you in writing first.

Who can see our data?

Other prospects cannot. Inside the product, only your own users can open your readings. Behind the product, one person has administrative access to the cloud project, Rahul Phaltankar, who could read stored files directly. That access is used only to run or support the beta.

Do you have a security certification or a penetration test?

No. We have tested our protections ourselves. There has been no independent security test and we hold no certification such as ISO 27001 or SOC 2. If your policy requires one, tell us now and we will say so plainly.

Is there a backup?

No separate backup copy. Your uploaded documents exist in one place. We keep earlier stored versions of files for 30 days to undo mistakes, after which an automatic rule removes them, but that is not a backup against losing the storage itself. Please keep your own copies of anything you upload.

What do you do if there is a breach?

We tell you without undue delay if we learn that someone has gained access to your data without permission. We have a short written procedure for the first hours, and the beta terms commit us to telling you.

Day 30 and after

What happens on day 30?

The beta closes on its own. Before then you can download your reading and fix list. We delete your workspace within 30 days after the beta ends unless you ask us in writing to keep it longer. You can ask us to delete it earlier, at any time, and we will do it within 14 days.

Does it turn into a paid service?

No, not automatically. The beta is free. If we ever offer a paid service, it will be a new written agreement that you can decline.

Practical

What support do we get, and how reliable is it?

Support is by email from Rahul Phaltankar, on a best-effort basis. It is a beta, so there is no uptime promise, and the first page load after a quiet spell can take a few extra seconds while the service wakes up.
