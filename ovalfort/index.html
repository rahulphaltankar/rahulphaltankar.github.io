Why it cannot wait

The first time someone asks should not be the first time you look.

A funding round, a large customer's security review and a regulator's letter all ask the same things. The law sets the ceiling on what a mistake can cost.

4%

EU and UK GDPR

Fines of up to 4% of worldwide annual turnover (UK: or £17.5m, whichever is higher; EU: or €20m).

7%

EU AI Act

Up to 7% of worldwide annual turnover, or €35m, for the most serious breaches.

₹250 cr

India DPDP Act

Penalties of up to ₹250 crore under the Act's schedule, for failing to take reasonable security safeguards.

6 hrs

India CERT-In

The Directions require certain cyber incidents to be reported within six hours of noticing them.

These are ceilings written into the law, not predictions about any company. Check the official texts for the exact conditions.

Step two: the deep dive

The one-pager is the thermometer. The deep dive is the blood test.

A voluntary, full reading of how your company actually works, tested against each law, with a written result you can act on and show.

  1. We open a private workspace for your company

    Your own sign-in. Your reading is kept apart from every other company's.

  2. You answer guided questions

    How you use AI, what personal data you hold, who processes it, how you handle a breach, and where your customers are. Plain questions, no legal training needed.

  3. You add your own evidence

    Policies, contracts, records. You upload what you have. Where you have nothing, the reading says so, because that is useful to know.

  4. You get a reading, law by law

    For each obligation that appears to apply: what it asks, whether your evidence meets it, where there is a gap, and where it cannot tell. Every finding links to the official text.

  5. You leave with a fix list

    Ordered by how serious the gap is, as a report and a spreadsheet you can hand to your team.

What gets tested, and where

Choose a law.

EU AI Act

  • Your role. Whether the Act treats you as a provider, a deployer or something else, because the duties differ.
  • Risk category. Whether anything you build or use falls into a prohibited, high-risk or transparency-only group.
  • Paperwork. The documentation, oversight and transparency duties that come with your category.

EU GDPR

  • Lawful basis and notices. Why you may process the data, and whether people are told.
  • Processors and transfers. Contracts with the suppliers that touch the data, and data leaving the EU.
  • People's rights and breaches. How you answer a request, and what you do in the first 72 hours of a breach.

UK GDPR

  • The same core duties under UK law. The UK has its own version, and its own regulator, the ICO.
  • UK transfer rules. Moving data out of the UK follows UK mechanisms, which differ from the EU's.
  • Where the two differ. The reading shows them side by side so you do not do the work twice.

India: DPDP Act and CERT-In Legal review pending

  • DPDP Act and Rules. Notice and consent, the rights of the people whose data you hold, breach reporting, and the extra duties for larger or higher-risk handlers.
  • CERT-In Directions. Incident reporting within six hours, keeping logs, and a named point of contact.
  • Honest status. The India part is built and sourced. An Indian advocate has not yet signed it off. We say so on every reading.

A missing answer is never "does not apply"

If the evidence is thin, the reading says it cannot tell. It never turns silence into comfort.

Citations are checked by code

No AI model decides whether you comply. Code reads your evidence against a stored copy of the law, and separate code checks that each quotation exists in the official text, word for word.

You can trace every line

Each finding points to a stored source and a dated record of how it was reached.

Where you can use it

A dated, sourced reading is easier to show than a promise.

Pick the room you are walking into.

In front of investors

Due diligence asks how you handle personal data and AI. Bring a dated reading and a list of what you are fixing, instead of saying you are on top of it. It is a better answer on a call, and a better document in a data room.

With large customers

Security and privacy questionnaires arrive before contracts do. A structured reading gives you consistent answers, with the source behind each, and shortens the back-and-forth.

If an authority writes

It shows you looked, when you looked, and what you did next. That is a record, not protection. It does not stop a regulator reaching its own view.

For your board and your team

The fix list is ordered by seriousness, so the first conversation is about the three things that matter most, not a hundred that do not.

Before a new market

Selling into the EU, the UK or India next? See what each one adds to your obligations before you commit the budget.

A differentiator, if you earn it

Most start-ups answer "are you compliant?" with a template. If your reading comes out strong, you can show a sourced record of how you got there. We do not certify anyone. The reading is yours to use as proof of work.

Why do it voluntarily

Because finding a gap yourself, in private, costs a few weeks of work. Finding it in a customer's review or a regulator's letter costs more, and you pick neither the date nor the audience.
